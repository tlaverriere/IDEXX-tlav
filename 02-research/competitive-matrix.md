# Competitive Matrix — Streak, Habit & Micro-Learning Apps

*Last updated: 2026-09-14*

## Method and confidence

Desk research against primary sources — company help centres, official blogs, SEC filings, App Store listings — checked 2026-09-14. Every figure below is labelled **verified** (primary source), **secondary** (credible third party), or **unverified** (widely repeated, not confirmable).

**Read the "Do not quote" list in §6 before taking any number into a meeting.** Pricing and mechanic details in this category change frequently and several widely-circulated figures could not be confirmed.

**Category exclusions per brief:** no social-media-first apps, no long-form course platforms (Coursera, Udemy, edX). Quizlet was assessed and rejected — its "streak" counts consecutive correct answers within a session, not days, so it is not comparable. Sololearn was rejected: no primary documentation of its streak mechanics exists.

## 1. The competitor set — and why these

| # | App | Why it's in | Comparability to Streakly |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Duolingo** | The category-defining streak product; sets user expectations everyone else inherits | **High** — same loop shape, though language-specific |
| 2 | **Babbel** | Direct contrast: same market, deliberately *less* streak-driven | **High** — shows the non-streak path to daily return |
| 3 | **Elevate** | Closest structural twin: pick a skill, short daily session, streak as the loop | **Highest** — and it has already solved our exact problem |
| 4 | **Brilliant** | Micro-learning with the most elegantly designed forgiveness mechanic found | **High**, with a caveat: markets a 20-minute session, not 5 |
| 5 | **Finch** | Not micro-learning, but the closest *problem* — it retrofitted a streak onto a brand built on non-punishment, and documented every tradeoff | **Medium on product, highest on our specific question** |
| — | **Lumosity** | Included as a **control case, not a peer**: unprotected streak, resets on first miss | **This is Streakly's current design at maturity** |

Lumosity earns its place by being what we already are. Its documented answer to a missed day is *"your streak will start over."* It is the do-nothing scenario, observable in a mature product.

## 2. Profiles

### Duolingo

- **Core features.** One lesson extends the streak — deliberately decoupled from the daily XP goal in 2020, which Duolingo reports moved D14 retention **+3.3%** and put **+19%** more new learners on a streak. *(Verified, [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak/).)*
- **Pricing.** US App Store IAPs for Super Duolingo span **$9.99–$119.99**, plus gem packs $0.99–$19.99. Apple does not publish durations, so **price-to-term mapping is unverified**. Notably **no Duolingo Max SKU appears**, consistent with the Q2 FY26 letter folding Video Call into Super. *(Verified as listed, [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/duolingo-language-lessons/id570060128); [Q2 FY26 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm).)*
- **Target customer.** Mass-market casual learners. **140.6M MAU, 58.7M DAU, 12.7M paid** (Q2 2026, verified).
- **Post-week-1 engagement.** *Streak Freeze* must be **bought with gems in advance** and auto-applies on a missed day; cap of 2 equipped is a 2022 figure, not re-confirmed for 2026. *Streak Repair* is purchasable with gems after a break. Milestone animations raised 7-day return **+1.7%**; users reaching a 7-day streak are **3.6x** more likely to finish a course. Notifications are selected per learner by a bandit algorithm trained on ~200M reminders. *(Verified. The "guilt-trip tone" is cultural reputation, not company-documented.)*
- **Notable recent changes.** Strategy shifted from monetization toward user growth with a stated **100M DAU** target; retention at an all-time-high 84%; **in-app purchase revenue down 23% YoY**; longer free trials. **In June 2026 Duolingo let lapsed users restore their longest-ever streak by completing three lessons: 15.4M learners revived streaks, roughly 8M of whom had no active streak at all.** Management called it evidence of "how large the opportunity is to bring former learners back" — and flagged it as **one-time**, not a standing mechanic. *(Verified, Q2 FY26 letter. This single datapoint is the most important thing in this document — see §5.)*

### Babbel

- **Core features.** Structured 10–15 minute lessons plus a spaced-repetition Review Manager. The streak counts **any** activity — lesson, vocab workout, podcast, AI conversation, grammar guide. *(Verified, [Babbel support](https://support.babbel.com/hc/en-us/articles/17020978582162-Streak).)*
- **Pricing.** US App Store, checked 2026-09-14: **1-month $17.99, 3-month $45.99, 12-month $89.99** single language, **$107.99** all-languages. **Group Plan $30.95/mo for up to 6, launched 2026-04-27** *(verified, [press release](https://www.babbel.com/press/en-us/releases/group-plan))*. Lifetime $299 and "$8.97/mo" framings are **unverified**.
- **Target customer.** Adults paying for structured, curriculum-led learning; >30M subscriptions sold (verified).
- **Post-week-1 engagement — the key contrast.** Babbel **gives 2 Streak Freezes free every week**, auto-applied, refreshed Mondays, non-rolling. Duolingo charges gems for the same protection; Babbel gifts it. Miss a day without one and the streak resets at local midnight. Daily return is driven by review-due notifications, user-set reminder timing, daily goals and badges — not by streak pressure. **No streak repair or comeback flow found** (absence by omission, not a confirmed "no").
- **Notable recent changes.** Babbel Speak AI voice trainer, open beta 2025-09-16. Group Plan April 2026. **No 2026 streak or retention mechanic change found.**

### Elevate *(The Mind Company)*

- **Core features.** A personalised "daily workout" of games plus a daily puzzle. Free tier = 3 games; paid = up to 5, with adjustable length. Streak credits on finishing the puzzle or the last game. *(Verified, [Elevate support](https://support.elevateapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/4402924805275-How-do-I-use-the-app).)* The commonly cited "10–15 min" session is **unverified**.
- **Pricing.** App Store, checked 2026-09-14: **$39.99/yr** (with 7- or 14-day trial variants), **$9.99/mo**, additional SKUs $4.99–$21.99. Elevate's own support page declines to publish prices.
- **Target customer.** Adults working on communication, reading, writing, math, memory, processing speed — skill-framed, not clinical.
- **Post-week-1 engagement — the best-designed in the set.** Streak freeze **auto-applies and the streak *pauses* rather than resets**. Every account **starts with 2**, max 2 held. Burn both and miss again → reset to 0 — **but two more are earned by reaching another 2-day streak**, so re-arming costs two days. Freezes are also purchasable, making the mechanic simultaneously a forgiveness system and a monetization surface. **Milestones at 3, 7, 14, 30, 50… so the first celebration lands on day 3.** A single daily reminder with user-set time; **no documented "you lost your streak" push**. *(All verified, [streak freeze help](https://support.elevateapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/28507604797595-What-is-a-streak-freeze).)*
- **Notable recent changes.** Rebranded Elevate Labs → **The Mind Company**, 2025-10-09. Launched sibling app **Spark** (daily knowledge puzzles, positioned explicitly to "make learning a daily habit"), 2025-11-12. Layered in Coins, Quests and **Leagues** (weekly XP ladders with promotion/demotion). *(Verified; "within 12 months" for the Leagues addition is inferred from article IDs — moderate confidence.)*

### Brilliant

- **Core features.** Interactive math, CS, science and AI lessons organised into Learning Paths. A day counts at **3 problems or 1 full lesson**. XP feeds **Leagues** — 30 learners per league, 10 tiers, weekly reset. **Markets a 20-minute session, not 5** — a meaningfully heavier daily ask than ours. *(Verified.)*
- **Pricing.** **$30/mo, $240/yr (~$20/mo)** on Brilliant's own site; group plans from **$33.99/mo for 3 seats + $10.10 per extra**. iOS IAPs span $24.99–$191.99 — **platform and regional variance is real, do not quote one number as global**. *(Verified, [Brilliant](https://brilliant.org/resources/choosing-brilliant/is-brilliant-worth-it/).)*
- **Target customer.** Students from roughly age 10 through college, adult lifelong learners, and K-12 teachers *(verified, Brilliant's own help centre)*.
- **Post-week-1 engagement — the most elegant mechanic found.** **Streak Charges**: earned **one per lesson completed**, max 2 held, never expire, **applied automatically**. A charge **preserves but does not extend** the streak. The design insight worth stealing: **earning is passive — a byproduct of normal use** — so most active users already hold protection at the moment they lapse. No purchase, no pre-arming, no decision required. Brilliant's own framing of a charge is *"a chance to apply what you've learned or take a break"* — **permission, not rescue** *(verified, official account)*. Out of charges → reset to 0. **Not established:** any comeback screen, win-back flow, retroactive repair, grace period, or first-milestone timing.
- **Notable recent changes.** **Koji**, an AI graphical tutor, launched **2026-05-29** — Socratic, sees the on-screen interactive, speaks proactively at lesson start to lower the barrier to asking for help. **No streak-mechanic changes found in the window.**

### Finch

- **Core features.** Complete goals → pet gains energy → returns from an adventure with a story and collectible currency; plus journaling, breathing and focus timers. *(Verified.)*
- **Pricing.** Finch Plus **$9.99/mo, $69.99/yr** *(verified, primary source)*.
- **Target customer.** Overwhelmed or burned-out adults, skewing women in their twenties *(secondary — [Slate, 2026-09-06](https://slate.com/technology/2026/09/finch-app-self-care-wellness-review.html))*. Consumer wellness, not education.
- **Post-week-1 engagement — three things nobody else does.** (1) **The streak advances on opening the app**, not on completing work — the bar to not-breaking is near zero. (2) **Repair is retroactive** — the break is fixed *after* it happens, using free savers earned every 3 adventures, max 2 held. (3) **Pause Mode** — the user declares an absence of N days; the streak is preserved and **goals and notifications are hidden** until manual unpause. Help copy states the intent as *"gentle consistency, not perfection."* Earliest documented reward moment: seasonal unlocks at **3 days**. *(Verified, [Finch help](https://help.finchcare.com/hc/en-us/articles/37780736136205-Understanding-Streaks), [Pause Mode](https://help.finchcare.com/hc/en-us/articles/37936144770701-Pause-Mode).)*
- **Notable recent changes.** Pause Mode shipped as the most-requested feature. A June 2026 bug broke streak repairs and affected streaks were auto-restored — **wiki-sourced, unverified**.

### Lumosity — control case

- **Core features.** Daily workout; free = 3 rotating games, Premium = 5-game workouts, 40+ games, cognitive testing.
- **Pricing.** App Store lists **$11.99–$89.00** but **tier labels are not disclosed**, so any monthly/annual/lifetime mapping is inference. Widely-blogged "$11.99/mo, $59.99/yr, $299.95 lifetime" is **unverified**.
- **Target customer.** Broad adult cognitive fitness, skewing older.
- **Post-week-1 engagement.** *"If you miss a day of training, your streak will start over."* **No freeze, pause, grace period, vacation mode or repair is documented** — confirmed against the current help article and the settings article. No streak-loss notification documented. Runs **"3-day Streak Challenge"** win-back emails *(medium confidence, captured marketing email)*.
- **Notable recent changes.** A ground-up rebuild added badges and **increased streak visibility** — investment went into making the streak *more prominent*, not more forgiving. Mindfulness removed. September 2026 release added Zen Mode.

## 4. What we're missing that is table stakes, not white space

Worth separating from the gaps below, because these are places we are simply **behind**, not places we could lead.

1. **Any protection at all.** Five of five real competitors ship some forgiveness mechanic. Only Lumosity — our control case — resets on first miss. We currently resemble the control, not the field.
2. **An early milestone.** Elevate celebrates on **day 3**. Duolingo measured milestone animations at **+1.7%** on 7-day return. Our own retained-user interview puts our first celebration at **day 30** — three weeks after the Day-7 window closes. This is the cheapest gap on the list to close and it is inside our metric's window.
3. **Cheap re-arming.** Elevate restores protection for a 2-day streak; Brilliant for one lesson. Nobody makes the road back expensive except us.

## 5. White space — two gaps nobody owns

### Gap 1 — The return visit. Almost nobody designs the session *after* a lapse, and nobody ships one as a standing surface.

Every competitor invests in **preventing** the break. Almost none design what happens when a user comes back anyway. The taxonomy in §3C reflects this: "designed return session" has no occupant as a standing product surface.

**Duolingo sized the prize for us.** In June 2026 they offered lapsed users their longest-ever streak back for three lessons in one sitting — streaks of 30+ days, 1–30 June. **15.4M learners revived a streak, roughly 8M of them with no active streak at all**, and revived-cohort retention ran better than a typical re-engaged cohort. Management's own words: evidence of "how large the opportunity is to bring former learners back."

**Corrected 2026-09-14 — this gap is narrower than first written.** An earlier draft of this document called the event a one-time campaign that "left the space open." That overstated it. Duolingo also publishes internal research on its "resurrected users," which points to a deliberate, ongoing investment in win-back rather than a single promotion. The honest reading: **the space is open, not empty.** It was run as a time-boxed event rather than a standing surface, by a company visibly studying the problem.

What survives the correction is the durable part: a dormant-user base responds to a specific, personal reason to return, and the reason that worked was **your own progress restored** — not generic encouragement. That is the shape of Lena's Comeback screen.

**The honest caveat, and it matters:** part of why this gap looks empty is that **no company documents its post-miss screen.** Both research passes flagged this independently — Elevate's and Lumosity's morning-after experience could not be verified from any source, and Brilliant's absence of a comeback screen is unconfirmed rather than confirmed. So "nobody owns this" is partly "desk research cannot see it." Before this claim goes to Marcus as a strategic bet, it needs **first-hand capture**: install four apps, break four streaks, screenshot the return. That is a few days of work and it converts the central argument of this document from inference to evidence.

### Gap 2 — Every mechanic protects the streak. None reframe the goal.

All six apps treat **consecutive days** as the objective, then bolt forgiveness onto it. Freezes, charges, shields and repairs are all patches on a goal that still defines a missed day as a failure to be absorbed.

Nobody in micro-learning ships a goal with **tolerance built into its definition** — where missing a day was always allowed for, so there is nothing to repair and nothing to forgive. Finch is the closest in spirit (*"gentle consistency, not perfection,"* streak advancing on app-open, Pause Mode hiding goals *and* notifications), but Finch is a wellness app and no learning product has taken the position.

This matters for us specifically because every patch mechanic is **reactive** — it fires at or after a break. Our own week-1 evidence says the harm starts *before* any break: a day-4 user disengaging with her streak fully intact. A freeze cannot reach her. A goal frame can, because it changes the target from day 1.

**This is the white space our own Candidate 2 (26-of-30) is aimed at** — and the competitive scan says no learning app currently occupies it.

## 7. Implication for our two candidates

The scan maps cleanly, and usefully, onto what is already on the table:

| White space | Our candidate | Read |
|---|---|---|
| **Gap 1 — the return visit** | Candidate 1, Comeback screen | Targets a genuinely unowned space that Duolingo has sized at ~8M dormant users responding to a personal reason to return |
| **Gap 2 — the goal frame** | Candidate 2, 26-of-30 | Targets a second unowned space, and is the only one of the two that can reach a pre-break week-1 user |

**This is an argument against bundling them.** They are bets on two *different* unowned positions, addressing two *different* failure modes — post-break dormancy and pre-break anxiety. Bundling them forfeits the ability to learn which white space is actually worth occupying, which is the attribution problem already logged in `../05-decide/experiment-26of30.md`.

**What the scan does not do** is validate either candidate. It says both target real gaps. It says nothing about whether those gaps are what is costing us nine points of Day-7 retention — that remains the open question Thursday exists to resolve.
