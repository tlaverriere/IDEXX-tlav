## FT1 — and it may not be a fast track at all

**Here is what users told us.** Not paraphrased:

- **Tom**, who churned at week 5: *"The app sent me this 'you lost your streak'
  notification that just made me feel bad."* You called this before it was a track:
  *"the 'you lost your streak' push has a brutal tone."*
- **NPS R4**: received **three notifications in one afternoon** and *"just turned them
  all off."*
- **NPS R2**: *"The first week was genuinely fun"* → *"after that the daily reminder
  just started to feel like nagging."*
- **From the sample data:** the current `streak_lost` notification has a **0% action
  rate across 141 sends.** There has never been anything to act on.

**The reframe, and it is the reason this is first.** We have been calling FT1 a
parallel track. It may be a **prerequisite**. The Comeback surface's only entry point
is a notification — QA found there is **no in-app fallback at all** — so a user who
has stopped trusting our pushes cannot reach the feature in any state. Tom's read of
the PRD would be: *the notification is why I left, and you put it out of scope.*

**So FT1 does not compete with the Comeback screen. It gates it.** That is an
argument for doing it first, not for doing more.

**What I am not doing:** writing the copy. The current notification text is the
product's, not a proposal from me. `prototype/index.html` has a PM-written lock-screen
line — treat it as a placeholder to replace, not a draft to edit.

## FT2 — start with the five-minute question, not the design

**Step 0, before any scoping: what is our current milestone schedule?**

The "our first celebration lands at day 30" figure is **an inference from a single
interview, and a loose one.** Priya said the 30-day celebration is what made the habit
stick; she never said nothing preceded it. We have been reasoning from one person's
recollection about something that is a matter of record.

**You or Raj can settle it in minutes, and the answer could dissolve this item or
sharpen it considerably.** Do not scope work before it is answered.

**If it holds, the primary-source case is good:**

- **Elevate's first milestone lands on day 3** (verified, their own help centre).
- **Duolingo measured milestone animations at +1.7% on 7-day return** (verified, their
  own blog).
- Both sit *inside* the Day-7 window. Ours, if the inference is right, lands three
  weeks past it.

**The overlap worth resolving early.** `design-review.md` Part 2 argues the highest-impact
week-1 change is a **day-1 reassurance moment** — *"You've got three spare days and a
freeze. Life happens."* — on audience arithmetic: it reaches every new user, where the
Comeback state reaches only week-1 users who break. **FT2 and that may be the same
work.** If they are, you have a head start and one thing to build rather than two. If
they are not, the difference is worth stating, because both are currently described as
"an acknowledgment inside week 1."

**The risk, stated because it is yours to judge:** congratulating a user on day 3 and
zeroing them on day 4 reads as tone-deaf and compounds the punishment framing your own
research documented. Cheap to build, cheap to get wrong.
